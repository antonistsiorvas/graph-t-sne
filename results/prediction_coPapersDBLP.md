## Expected differences from this model

- **The walk is still the largest phase** (79% of the sequential time), but P grows 16.5x while the walk grows 1.6x, so P's share rises from 2% to 17%.
- **P and attraction may be faster per nonzero than predicted.** The median degree is 34 (com-Amazon: 4), so the per-row overhead (loop setup, the min/max pass, reading y_i) is shared by 8x more entries. Risk the other way: the matrix (over 1 GB with P, Pc and mirror) streams from memory, as com-Amazon's (74 MB) already did.
- **P's speedup is capped by the sequential mirror.** At 12 threads the mirror is predicted at 195 ms of P's 499 ms (OpenMP).
- **Attraction: degree imbalance.** Rows of degree up to 3,299 in chunks of 256 rows; if high-degree rows are clustered in the numbering, one chunk can be much heavier than the rest and finish last.
- **Walk: the shape of the embedding.** Terms per point are assumed equal to com-Amazon's (584). A more clustered Y0 gives crowded leaves and more terms.
- **Tiny phases** (build, centers of mass, gradient) are dominated by thread overhead at 12 threads, which does not grow with n; scaling them by the work overestimates them, but they are a few ms either way.
- **Memory**: about 1.3 GB for the matrix arrays; the machine has 7.6 GB.
